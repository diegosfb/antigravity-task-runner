# Pitch-deck strategy

## Select a narrative spine

Use only the slides needed to earn the requested decision.

| Deck | Typical spine |
|---|---|
| Investor/fundraising | Problem, insight, solution, product, market, traction, model, go-to-market, competition, team, financial logic, milestones, ask |
| Sales/product | Customer problem, cost of status quo, solution, proof, differentiation, value, implementation, commercial next step |
| Partnership | Strategic fit, complementary capabilities, joint customer value, proposed model, proof, responsibilities, economics, next step |

Move exceptional evidence earlier. A deck with strong traction may lead with it; a technical breakthrough may lead with the insight. Put detailed diligence material in an appendix when it interrupts the main argument.

## Slide tests

Every slide should answer:

1. What single claim should the audience remember?
2. Why does it matter to this audience now?
3. What evidence makes it credible?
4. Why is this the right visual form?
5. How does it advance the eventual ask?

Prefer assertive titles such as “Enterprise retention reached 118%” over labels such as “Traction.” Keep body text sparse enough to read at presentation distance. Use screenshots only when the important interaction remains legible.

## Evidence discipline

- Use bottom-up market logic alongside any top-down estimate.
- Show absolute values with growth rates so a small base is not disguised.
- Treat projections as projections and expose their assumptions.
- Compare against real alternatives, including manual work and doing nothing.
- Use customer logos and quotations only with permission and exact attribution.
- Make the ask specific: decision, amount or commitment, use, timing, and resulting milestone.
