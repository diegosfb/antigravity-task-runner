# Narrative frameworks

Choose one primary framework; combining several usually creates repetition.

## Problem–evidence–solution–proof–ask

The reliable default. Establish a costly problem, show evidence that existing alternatives fail, present the differentiated solution, prove adoption or effectiveness, then make the ask.

## Before–after–bridge

Best for sales and transformation pitches. Contrast the customer’s current state with a measurable future state, then show how the product and implementation bridge the gap.

## Insight or epiphany

Best when a non-obvious insight creates the advantage. Explain conventional wisdom, what changed or was missed, the new approach, and evidence that the insight is correct.

## Strategic-fit narrative

Best for partnerships. Establish shared customer or market pressure, complementary assets, the joint proposition, operating model, economics, risks, and a concrete pilot or next decision.

## Narrative validation

- The first three slides establish audience relevance and the central tension.
- Each slide creates a reason for the next; remove slides that merely repeat.
- Evidence appears near the claim it supports.
- Likely objections are answered before the ask.
- The strongest proof is not buried.
- The ask follows logically from the story and states what happens next.

Do not use emotional storytelling to conceal weak evidence. Preserve meaningful uncertainty and counterevidence.
