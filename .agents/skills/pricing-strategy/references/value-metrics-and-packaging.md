# Value metrics and packaging

## Decision order

Make pricing decisions in this order:

1. Value metric: what scales with customer value.
2. Packaging: which capabilities, limits, and service levels belong together.
3. Price point: the amount charged for each package and usage band.

Do not compensate for a weak value metric with additional tiers or discounts.

## Selecting a value metric

Evaluate candidates such as seats, usage, outcomes, features, a flat fee, or a
hybrid base-plus-variable model. Prefer a metric that:

- Correlates with value received and grows with customer success.
- Is predictable and understandable before purchase.
- Is measurable, auditable, and difficult to game.
- Supports the product's gross-margin and delivery economics.
- Fits procurement expectations and the actual buying unit.

Test counterexamples. Seats perform poorly when one operator creates most of the
value; raw usage can create bill shock; flat fees subsidize heavy users; outcome
pricing requires attributable, contractible outcomes.

## Tier architecture

Good-better-best is a useful starting hypothesis, not a universal rule. Use a
different number of public packages when customer evidence, product structure,
or sales motion supports it.

For each tier, define:

- Target segment and job to be done.
- Included core value and intentional limits.
- Usage or seat allowance and overage behavior.
- Integrations, reporting, administration, security, support, and SLA.
- Upgrade trigger tied to customer success rather than artificial frustration.
- Expected realized price, cost to serve, and gross margin.

Keep tier names immediately understandable. Enterprise packaging should reflect
real enterprise requirements; do not add security controls merely as artificial
gates when they are necessary for safe product use.

## Freemium versus trial

Choose freemium when ongoing free use creates distribution, network, data, or
product-led acquisition benefits and marginal cost is controlled. Choose a
trial when customers need to experience a paid workflow, the market is focused,
or free usage would impose material cost or support burden.

Any free plan must deliver a coherent outcome. Its upgrade boundary should be a
natural success threshold, and the economics must include infrastructure,
support, abuse, and conversion opportunity cost.
