# Pricing pages and positioning

## Page specification

A pricing-page specification should make the purchase decision understandable:

- Clear package names, target customer, price basis, billing period, and CTA.
- A short list of meaningful differentiators rather than every feature.
- Explicit usage limits, overage behavior, contract term, and cancellation
  implications.
- Monthly and annual choices where both exist, with savings stated accurately.
- A full comparison, common objections, relevant proof, and truthful compliance
  or security claims below the initial package summary.

Do not fabricate badges, certifications, customer logos, testimonials, savings,
or "most popular" claims. Highlight a recommended plan only when the business
has intentionally selected it for a defined segment.

## Enterprise presentation

Publish a price when the enterprise package is standardized. Use a starting
price or contact-sales motion when scope, implementation, usage, support, or
contract terms materially change the quote. Preserve enough transparency for a
buyer to determine fit before entering a sales process.

## Positioning

Map products across dimensions buyers actually use, such as price, capability,
service, implementation effort, specialization, or risk. "Premium," "value,"
and "disruptor" are strategic hypotheses that require evidence in messaging,
product experience, and delivery economics.

Avoid percentage-based claims about how far above or below the market to price
unless supported by a current, comparable dataset. Competitor pages can change;
record URLs and observation dates when conducting research.

## Experimentation

Test comprehension as well as conversion. Track plan selection, checkout
completion, qualified pipeline, realized price, discounting, activation,
retention, contraction, expansion, support load, and margin by segment.
Predefine guardrails and avoid simultaneously changing price, packaging,
positioning, and acquisition mix when causal learning matters.
