# Solo Founder Prioritization

Turn an overloaded founder's choices into a short, defensible action sequence. Optimize scarce time, money, and attention against the founder's actual goals and constraints—not a generic startup persona.

## Establish the decision frame

Identify:

- business model, stage, customers, current traction, and near-term objective;
- founder capabilities, available hours, energy constraints, cash, runway, and external commitments;
- candidate tasks or decisions, deadlines, dependencies, and reversibility;
- current bottleneck and evidence for it;
- security, privacy, legal, regulatory, financial, accessibility, contractual, customer-harm, and continuity constraints;
- work that only the founder can perform and resources available for delegation or purchase.

Do not assume the founder is technical, bootstrapped, building SaaS, seeking venture funding, or optimizing only for revenue. If key inputs are missing, state provisional assumptions and avoid false precision.

## Normalize the work

Convert each candidate into an outcome rather than an activity. For example, replace “redesign landing page” with the measurable uncertainty or result the redesign is expected to address.

For each item capture only decision-relevant fields:

- intended outcome and affected stakeholder;
- evidence and confidence;
- estimated founder time, elapsed time, cash, and coordination cost;
- expected benefit and when it may appear;
- dependency or deadline;
- reversibility and cost of delay;
- risk if performed, deferred, or omitted;
- whether founder judgment is genuinely required.

Use ranges when estimates are uncertain. Do not invent revenue impact, probabilities, conversion rates, or hourly values.

## Apply the decision ladder

Stop at the first option that adequately achieves or tests the outcome:

1. **Eliminate:** Is the outcome unnecessary, unsupported, duplicated, or no longer aligned?
2. **Defer:** Can it wait without unacceptable cost, compounding risk, or lost option value?
3. **Reuse:** Does an existing asset, process, product capability, relationship, or prior decision cover it?
4. **Simplify:** What is the smallest action that resolves the bottleneck or tests the critical assumption?
5. **Automate:** Is the task stable and repeated enough that automation will repay its creation and maintenance cost?
6. **Delegate:** Can another person perform it with clear inputs, authority, quality criteria, and review cost?
7. **Buy:** Does a product or service solve it more safely and cheaply than building or learning it?
8. **Founder executes:** Keep it with the founder only when unique judgment, trust, accountability, or learning makes that necessary.

Automation and delegation are not automatically efficient. Include setup, review, exception handling, vendor risk, and maintenance.

## Prioritize

Use a qualitative or range-based comparison suited to the available evidence. Consider:

- contribution to the current objective or bottleneck;
- urgency, deadline, and cost of delay;
- confidence and learning value;
- founder time, cash, and attention required;
- reversibility and option value;
- dependencies and work unlocked;
- risk reduction and mandatory obligations.

Compliance, security, accessibility, contractual obligations, data-loss prevention, customer safety, payroll, taxes, and critical service reliability are constraints or mandatory work—not low-value overhead to discard because their immediate revenue impact is unclear.

Expected-value calculations may support comparison only when inputs are stated and defensible. Show ranges and sensitivity; never let a speculative dollar estimate manufacture certainty.

## Choose the horizon

Match the output to the decision:

- **Today:** one critical outcome, essential operating obligations, and at most a small number of supporting actions.
- **One or two weeks:** a focused execution sequence with capacity buffer and explicit stop conditions.
- **Month or quarter:** objectives, evidence milestones, resource allocation, and decisions—not an exhaustive task list.
- **Single decision:** recommendation, alternatives, reversibility, evidence gap, and next test.

Do not fill available capacity to 100%. Preserve realistic buffer for support, incidents, administration, and new information.

## Output

Return:

1. objective, horizon, constraints, and current bottleneck;
2. assumptions and confidence;
3. ranked actions with outcome, owner, effort range, rationale, and completion or stop condition;
4. `DO NOW`, `NEXT`, `DEFER`, `DELEGATE`, `AUTOMATE`, `BUY`, and `STOP` decisions only where relevant;
5. what was deliberately skipped and why;
6. risks, mandatory safeguards, and capacity buffer;
7. the next review trigger or date.

For a complex request, recommend the smallest safe version and state what it omits. If the founder explicitly chooses the fuller version after seeing the trade-off, support that choice without repeating the argument.

## Boundaries

- Prioritization does not authorize purchases, hiring, contracting, outreach, publication, production changes, fundraising, or deletion of data or work.
- Do not cut validation, error handling, security, privacy, accessibility, legal compliance, financial controls, data protection, or real edge cases to save time.
- Do not make medical or mental-health judgments about workload, energy, stress, or burnout.
- Use current evidence when vendor capabilities, costs, laws, markets, or platform rules materially affect a recommendation.
