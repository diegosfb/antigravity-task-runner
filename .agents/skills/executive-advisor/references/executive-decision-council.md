# Executive Decision Council

Use this mode when one material decision needs independently developed,
cross-functional perspectives before a human decision. It is a deliberation
method—not a legal board meeting, board vote, governance record, or substitute
for directors and executives exercising their real duties.

## Entry criteria

Use the council when the decision spans multiple executive domains, disagreement
or groupthink risk is material, and the cost of a normal single-perspective
answer is meaningful. Do not use it merely to make an ordinary decision look
important. A user may request a lighter two-perspective comparison.

Establish the decision, actual decision owner, deadline, options already under
consideration, constraints, evidence, affected stakeholders, reversibility,
approval requirements, and what would justify deferral.

## Deliberation

1. **Context and roles:** Select two to four perspectives whose decision rights
   or expertise genuinely differ. Include a missing stakeholder or customer
   perspective when material. Do not simulate a full executive roster.
2. **Independent views:** Develop each view from the common evidence before
   exposing it to the other positions. For each, state recommendation,
   evidence, assumptions, risks, confidence, and what would change the view.
   Do not reveal private chain-of-thought; provide concise decision rationale.
3. **Critic pass:** Examine suspicious consensus, shared assumptions, omitted
   voices, domain overreach, weak evidence, downside, reversibility, opportunity
   cost, and implementation burden. Steelman minority positions rather than
   forcing disagreement.
4. **Synthesis:** Present genuine agreement, unresolved disagreements, the
   decision criterion behind each disagreement, options and trade-offs, a
   recommended choice when evidence supports one, and conditions for reversal.
5. **Human gate:** Stop for the actual decision owner to approve, modify,
   reject, request more evidence, or defer. Advice does not become a decision
   through silence or elapsed time, and the agent may challenge the choice
   respectfully when a material risk remains.
6. **Decision record:** Only when explicitly authorized, record the decision,
   rationale, dissent, assumptions, owner, actions, due dates, review date, and
   reversal conditions in the user-designated location. Do not invent a memory
   path, formal minutes, board resolution, or `DO_NOT_RESURFACE` policy.

## Output

- Decision and owner
- Perspectives selected and why
- Independent position summaries
- Critic findings
- Agreements and unresolved disagreements
- Options, trade-offs, and recommendation
- Missing evidence and deferral option
- Human decision status
- Proposed decision record and actions, if approved

## Boundaries

- Call this an executive decision council unless real directors are actually
  participating under the organization's governance process.
- Do not imply fiduciary, board, shareholder, legal, employment, investment,
  financial, security, or regulatory approval.
- Do not guarantee that isolated model outputs are independent people or
  eliminate groupthink; the method only reduces anchoring within the analysis.
- Actual agendas, pre-reads, minutes, written consents, resolutions, notices,
  quorum, voting, and corporate records require a separate board-governance
  workflow and qualified legal/corporate-secretary review.
