# Team archetype benchmarks

Use these as diagnostic starting ranges, not targets.

## Product / digital application pod
Typical 7-10 person unit: 1 tech lead or senior lead; 4-6 software engineers across FE/BE/full-stack; 1-2 QA/SDET depending on automation maturity and risk; 0.5-1 product/BA; shared UX, architecture, DevOps/SRE, and delivery leadership as appropriate.

## Backend / API / platform pod
Bias toward backend/platform engineers. FE may be zero. Include platform/SRE/DevOps when teams own deployment/runtime. QA can be embedded SDET or engineering-owned depending on test strategy.

## Frontend-heavy experience pod
FE can exceed BE when APIs/platforms already exist. Ensure product/UX capacity and integration ownership. Avoid interpreting a high FE ratio as automatically wrong.

## Data / AI team
Composition depends on lifecycle: data engineering, ML/AI engineering, platform/MLOps, domain/product, evaluation/QA, and architecture. Do not apply application-development FE/BE ratios.

## Modernization / migration
Early phases need more architecture, analysis, archaeology, platform, and test strategy. Migration factories can later use more repeatable mid-level execution. Preserve legacy knowledge and validation capacity.

## Managed service / operations
Use coverage model, service levels, incident volume, skill concurrency, shift structure, automation, and escalation tiers rather than product-pod ratios.

## Ratio diagnostics
- PM/delivery leadership: inspect span and coordination complexity rather than enforcing a fixed ratio.
- QA: higher manual/regulatory/legacy risk may require more; mature automated quality engineering may require fewer dedicated testers.
- BA/product: higher ambiguity, domain complexity, stakeholder count, or requirements intensity increases need.
- Architecture: prefer shared architecture with empowered senior engineers unless scale/complexity warrants dedicated architects.
