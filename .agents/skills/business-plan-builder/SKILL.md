# Business Plan Builder

Build a decision-useful business plan whose market, operating, and financial sections agree with one another. Tailor the plan to its audience and maturity; do not generate a ceremonial document filled with invented precision.

## Establish the planning contract

Identify:

- concept, customer, painful job, solution, stage, geography, industry, and business model;
- plan audience, decision it supports, required format, time horizon, and level of detail;
- founders, team capabilities, distribution access, constraints, capital preference, and relevant advantages;
- existing customer evidence, traction, revenue, costs, research, product artifacts, and financial records;
- target outcomes, milestones, reviewers, confidentiality constraints, and applicable legal or regulatory requirements.

Founder profile, location, funding strategy, hiring geography, and revenue targets are inputs—not defaults. Never assume a San Francisco or Argentina connection, a software/IoT background, a US-first strategy, bootstrap funding, or a three-year `$1M ARR` target unless supplied.

If evidence is incomplete, state assumptions and build a preliminary plan around validation milestones. Do not disguise missing evidence as market research.

## Select one mode

- **Lean plan:** concise problem, customer, solution, advantage, business model, acquisition approach, economics, risks, and next experiments. Use for early decisions and iteration.
- **Investor plan:** opportunity, evidence, market, competition, defensibility, growth model, team, milestones, scenarios, capital need, and use of funds. Distinguish ambition from validated traction.
- **Lender plan:** company and owner background, operating model, demand evidence, historical performance where available, repayment capacity, collateral or guarantees when applicable, cash flow, downside coverage, and funding use. Do not present investor-style upside as repayment evidence.
- **Internal operating plan:** strategic choices, annual or quarterly objectives, initiatives, owners, resources, dependencies, operating metrics, budget, risks, and review cadence.

Use a custom or hybrid structure when the intended reader requires it. Include only sections that support the decision or satisfy a stated submission requirement.

## Evidence ledger

Classify material inputs and claims as:

- `VERIFIED`: supported by supplied records or an attributable source;
- `MANAGEMENT-PROVIDED`: supplied internally but not independently verified;
- `ESTIMATE`: calculated from disclosed inputs;
- `HYPOTHESIS`: requires validation;
- `UNKNOWN`: unavailable and not safely inferable.

Keep external market evidence, management assumptions, and model outputs visibly separate. Cite current sources near time-sensitive claims and record their publication or retrieval dates where useful.

Prefer primary sources such as regulators, government statistics, company filings, official product and pricing pages, standards bodies, and original industry research. Use secondary aggregators only when primary evidence is unavailable, and describe the limitation. Never treat a top-down TAM estimate as reachable demand.

## Build the plan

Choose from the following modules based on the selected mode:

### Business and customer

- Executive summary aligned with the detailed plan and model.
- Problem, current alternatives, urgency, and evidence of willingness to change or pay.
- Product or service, customer outcome, scope, delivery model, and validation status.
- Narrow initial customer segment, buyer, user, buying trigger, decision process, and expansion path.

### Market and competition

- Bottom-up reachable market using customer counts, price or spend, geography, eligibility, and channel constraints.
- TAM/SAM/SOM only when useful to the audience, with definitions, sources, and assumptions.
- Direct competitors, indirect alternatives, manual/internal substitutes, pricing evidence, positioning, and likely competitive response.
- Differentiation that matters to the buyer and a realistic assessment of distribution, switching costs, data, network, brand, regulatory, or technical defensibility.

### Commercial model

- Revenue model, value metric, packaging, price assumptions, sales motion, contracting path, and retention or repeat-purchase drivers.
- Go-to-market phases tied to the customer journey, concrete channels, owners, costs, conversion assumptions, and evidence.
- Marketing message and channel plan proportional to the business; do not mandate a fixed number of channels.
- Partnerships or platform dependencies only when their incentives and feasibility are credible.

### Operations and execution

- Product, service, technology, supply, support, compliance, and quality requirements relevant to delivery.
- Milestones tied to measurable evidence or operating outcomes rather than activity alone.
- Team responsibilities, capability gaps, hiring or vendor triggers, timing, and fully loaded cost assumptions.
- Material dependencies, constraints, decisions, and risk mitigations with owners and early warning indicators.

### Financial model

Use historical records when available and clearly distinguish them from projections. Model from operational drivers such as:

- customer or transaction volume;
- price, mix, discounts, and revenue recognition timing;
- acquisition, conversion, churn, retention, expansion, and sales-cycle assumptions;
- cost of goods or service delivery, gross margin, headcount, infrastructure, sales and marketing, general administration, taxes, working capital, and capital expenditures;
- funding events, debt service, cash runway, and minimum cash requirements.

Show the arithmetic and units. Include conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios when uncertainty is material. Identify the assumptions with the greatest effect and provide sensitivity analysis where decision-useful.

Do not invent CAC, LTV, churn, growth, price, salary, margin, or financing terms. Use explicit placeholders or hypothesis ranges when evidence is missing. Projections are scenarios, not forecasts or guarantees.

## Reconcile the plan

Before delivery, verify that:

- customer volumes, prices, revenue, channel capacity, staffing, delivery capacity, and cash requirements reconcile across sections;
- milestones and hiring triggers follow from the operating and financial assumptions;
- the funding request equals evidenced cash needs plus an explicit buffer and supports the stated milestones;
- the downside case exposes liquidity, repayment, concentration, and execution risks;
- every material number has a source, input, formula, or labelled assumption;
- the executive summary contains no claim contradicted by the plan;
- unknowns and validation needs remain visible.

## Output

Return:

1. plan mode, audience, decision, scope, and planning date;
2. evidence ledger and major assumptions;
3. the tailored business plan;
4. financial scenarios and sensitivities appropriate to the available evidence;
5. key risks, owners, mitigations, and validation milestones;
6. unresolved information and required reviews;
7. source list with dates for external evidence.

If the evidence cannot support a credible full plan, produce a clearly labelled preliminary plan and a prioritized evidence-gathering checklist instead of filling gaps with plausible numbers.

## Boundaries

- Planning does not authorize incorporation, fundraising, lending applications, spending, hiring, outreach, publication, contracting, or product implementation.
- Do not imply investment suitability, credit approval, legal compliance, tax treatment, or guaranteed returns.
- Material tax, securities, lending, employment, privacy, regulatory, and contractual matters require qualified review.
- Protect confidential business, customer, employee, and financial information; do not upload or disclose it without authorization.
