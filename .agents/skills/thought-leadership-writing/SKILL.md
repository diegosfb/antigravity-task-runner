# Thought Leadership Writing

Turn an expert's real point of view, experience, and evidence into a credible article or platform-native publication. Create a publication-ready draft; publication remains a separate, explicitly authorized action.

## Establish the editorial contract

Identify the author or organization, audience, subject, actual thesis, intended publication, desired outcome, evidence, source material, tone examples, length, byline, disclosure requirements, and reviewers. If the author has not supplied a genuine viewpoint, help frame possible positions as options and obtain their choice before attributing one to them.

Separate:

- `VERIFIED` facts supported by supplied or authoritative sources;
- `AUTHOR-PROVIDED` experience and opinion;
- `INFERENCE` that must be presented as analysis;
- `REVIEW-REQUIRED` legal, employer, client, partner, confidentiality, security, financial, regulatory, or reputational claims;
- `UNKNOWN` details that require a placeholder or omission.

Never fabricate personal experience, customer stories, credentials, affiliations, quotations, research, metrics, outcomes, or organizational positions.

## Choose the mode

- **Thought-leadership article:** a distinctive thesis, useful tension, evidence, implications, and practical conclusion.
- **Technical article:** an accurate explanation or argument supported by concrete architecture, code, data, experiments, or operational experience. Validate technical claims and examples.
- **Opinion essay:** clearly distinguish judgment from fact, represent counterarguments fairly, and avoid presenting a proposed view as the author's settled belief.
- **Corporate publication:** align with supplied editorial and brand guidance while retaining substance; route material organizational claims to appropriate reviewers.
- **LinkedIn article or post:** adapt the idea to the requested format, use a strong first line and short readable sections, and avoid engagement bait, hashtag spam, or pasting a long-form article unchanged.
- **Adaptation:** preserve the source thesis and facts while changing structure, length, examples, and call to action for the target publication.

## Draft

1. Build a compact thesis and evidence outline. Each section should advance the argument rather than repeat it.
2. Lead with a concrete observation, tension, example, result, or question. Avoid generic trend openings and promotional throat-clearing.
3. Use the author's terminology and sentence rhythm when representative samples are supplied. Otherwise default to clear, specific, low-hype prose.
4. Attribute external facts and distinguish evidence from interpretation. Use current authoritative sources when time-sensitive claims materially affect the article.
5. Include code, diagrams, data, or examples only when they improve understanding and are safe and authorized to disclose.
6. End with a conclusion or practical implication that follows from the argument; add a call to action only when appropriate to the publication and goal.
7. Adapt the title, deck/excerpt, headings, length, links, tags, and formatting to the target publication's current requirements.

## Editorial review

Before delivery:

- verify names, roles, dates, figures, links, quotations, technical claims, and source attribution;
- check that the draft expresses the author's supplied view rather than a plausible invented persona;
- remove filler, clichés, unsupported superlatives, fake certainty, and concealed promotional copy;
- check confidentiality, employer/client attribution, copyright, trademark, security, and disclosure concerns;
- flag every unresolved fact or approval dependency;
- return the draft with a concise publication checklist and optional platform-specific variants requested by the user.

## Boundaries

- Do not publish, schedule, upload, email, or modify an external platform without separate explicit authorization at the time of the action.
- Do not impersonate an author or imply their approval. The named author or organization must review attributed opinions before publication.
- Do not provide undisclosed ghostwritten testimonials, deceptive endorsements, fabricated consensus, plagiarism, or citation laundering.
- Preserve confidential, embargoed, personal, client, partner, and security-sensitive information.
- Use `press-release` for formal news announcements intended for media distribution. Use this skill when the primary artifact advances an expert argument, interpretation, or educational point of view.
