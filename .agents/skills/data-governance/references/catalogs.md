# Data Catalogs

Use when the request concerns discovery, metadata management, catalog selection, or adoption.

## Define the outcome first

Identify who must find what, which decisions metadata supports, and how freshness and ownership will be maintained. A catalog without accountable owners and ingestion operations becomes stale inventory.

## Minimum metadata contract

- Stable asset identifier and platform location
- Business name, description, domain, and glossary terms
- Technical and business owner plus escalation route
- Classification, criticality, and lifecycle state
- Producer, consumers, and supported-use constraints
- Schema and change history
- Lineage and quality links
- Access-request path and policy references
- Last observed and last reviewed timestamps

## Selection criteria

Evaluate current capabilities rather than relying on a frozen product comparison:

- Source and transformation integrations
- Push, pull, and event ingestion
- Table and column lineage coverage
- Search, glossary, ownership, and collaboration workflows
- Policy, classification, quality, and contract integration
- API and automation support
- Identity, authorization, tenancy, and audit controls
- Operating effort, deployment model, portability, and cost

Use a time-boxed pilot with representative sources, owners, and consumers. Verify metadata freshness, search success, ownership response, and change handling before platform-wide adoption.

## Operations

Assign ingestion owners, freshness objectives, failure alerts, schema-evolution handling, deprecation rules, and periodic owner attestation. Measure active use and successful discovery—not merely asset count.
