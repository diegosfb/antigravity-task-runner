# Ownership and Operating Model

Use for roles, decision rights, governance forums, maturity assessment, rollout, and measurement.

## Roles

Define roles according to the organization rather than assuming titles:

- **Business owner:** accountable for meaning, permitted use, value, and risk decisions
- **Steward:** maintains metadata, classification, issue triage, and policy operations
- **Technical owner:** operates pipelines, schemas, controls, and service objectives
- **Control owner:** designs and attests a governance or security control
- **Consumer:** uses data within the contract and reports issues or change impact

Use a RACI or decision-rights matrix for access, classification, contract changes, retention, quality incidents, exceptions, and deprecation. Every critical asset needs a resolvable accountable owner, not merely a team label.

## Operating cadence

- Owner and classification attestation
- Access recertification
- Contract and deprecation review
- Governance exception review
- Metadata and lineage coverage review
- Policy effectiveness and incident review
- Prioritized remediation planning

## Maturity

Assess observable capabilities rather than assigning a flattering level. Typical progression is:

1. Inventory critical assets and establish ownership.
2. Standardize metadata, classification, and access workflow.
3. Add lineage, contracts, control evidence, and recurring reviews.
4. Automate high-confidence controls and measure effectiveness.

Do not deploy every governance capability at once. Pilot one representative domain with committed owners and consumers, then expand from measured results.

## Measures

Use a balanced set such as critical assets with accountable owners, metadata freshness, successful discovery, lineage coverage for priority flows, access-review completion, contract adoption, exception age, request turnaround, and recurring incidents. Counts alone do not prove governance effectiveness.
