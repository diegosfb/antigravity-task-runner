# Research synthesis

## Prepare the evidence

Inventory each source with participant or event identifier, method, segment,
date, context, and limitations. Remove unnecessary personal data and separate
direct evidence from researcher interpretation. Do not convert assumptions into
quotes, behaviors, or measured findings.

Use a consistent coding frame while allowing new codes to emerge. Useful
starting codes include goals, behaviors, context, pain points, workarounds,
expectations, outcomes, and direct quotes.

## Synthesis workflow

1. Code observations at the smallest useful evidence unit.
2. Cluster related evidence without discarding contradictions or outliers.
3. Name each theme as a neutral observation before proposing a cause.
4. Record supporting and disconfirming evidence, source coverage, affected
   segments, and uncertainty.
5. Separate finding, interpretation, opportunity, and recommendation.
6. Validate important themes against raw evidence and, where possible, another
   researcher or participant group.

## Traceability contract

Each finding should include:

- Finding statement.
- Evidence references and representative verbatim quotes.
- Number and type of supporting sources, with the denominator where meaningful.
- Contradictory evidence and sampling limitations.
- Affected users, journey stage, and observable impact.
- Confidence stated as a reasoned judgment, not inferred from sample size alone.
- Opportunity or recommendation labeled separately from the evidence.

Never fabricate a quote or assign one participant's statement to a segment.
Preserve participant anonymity and consent constraints in shared artifacts.

## Prioritization

Prioritize opportunities using criteria suited to the decision, such as
frequency, severity, breadth, strategic importance, confidence, effort, risk,
and reversibility. Define every scale before scoring. A formula is a decision
aid, not objective truth; retain the underlying evidence and judgment.

## Evidence strength

Sample-size heuristics do not establish validity by themselves. Confidence also
depends on recruitment quality, segment coverage, method fit, saturation,
measurement reliability, triangulation, and consistency. Quantitative
comparisons or experiments need an analysis plan appropriate to the expected
effect and decision risk.

## Output

Produce a concise synthesis containing research scope, methods, participants,
limitations, themes, traceable findings, contradictions, prioritized
opportunities, unanswered questions, and recommended next research. Link every
material claim back to evidence that reviewers can inspect.
